User Guide

How to Reconcile a Bank Account in QuickBooks Online

Written by Brittany Nation. Applies to QuickBooks Online (all plans with bank accounts connected or entered manually).

What this guide does

Reconciling means proving that the balance in QuickBooks Online matches the balance at your bank, transaction by transaction, for a set period. When an account is reconciled, you know that every deposit and payment on the bank statement is recorded once, at the right amount, and that nothing is missing or entered twice.

This guide walks you through one full reconciliation of a bank account, from opening the Reconcile page to a difference of $0.00. It ends with fixes for the three problems that stop most reconciliations: the difference will not reach zero, a transaction is missing, and the beginning balance is wrong.

Before you begin

Gather these items first. The reconciliation goes much faster when nothing has to be hunted down halfway through.

  • The bank statement for the period you are reconciling, opened or printed. You need the statement ending date and the statement ending balance.
  • Access to QuickBooks Online with permission to view and edit the bank account.
  • All transactions for the period entered in QuickBooks, including deposits, checks, debit card purchases, and bank fees.
  • The ending balance from your last reconciliation, if this account has been reconciled before. QuickBooks carries it forward as the beginning balance.
Tip: Reconcile one month at a time, in order. If an account has never been reconciled, or has fallen behind, start with the oldest month and work forward. Skipping a month makes the next one much harder to balance.

Procedure

  1. Open the Reconcile page. Select the Settings gear icon at the top right of QuickBooks Online, then choose Reconcile under the Tools column. You can also go to Accounting, then Chart of accounts, find the account, and choose Reconcile from the action column.
  2. Choose the account. On the Reconcile page, select the bank account you want to reconcile from the Account dropdown. QuickBooks shows the date of the last reconciliation for that account, if there is one.
  3. Enter the statement information. In the Statement ending date field, enter the ending date printed on your bank statement. In the Ending balance field, enter the ending balance from the statement exactly as printed, including cents. The Beginning balance field fills in automatically. Check that it matches the beginning balance on your statement before you continue.
  4. Start reconciling. Select Start reconciling. QuickBooks lists every unreconciled transaction in the account up to the statement ending date, with payments on one side and deposits on the other.
  5. Match the transactions on your statement. Working down your bank statement one line at a time, find each transaction in the list and select the checkbox next to it. A checked transaction is treated as cleared at the bank. Check off deposits first, then payments, or follow the statement order; what matters is that every line on the statement gets checked, and nothing that is not on the statement gets checked. If a transaction in QuickBooks combines several statement lines, such as a batch deposit, check the total and confirm the amounts add up.
  6. Watch the difference. At the top of the page, QuickBooks tracks the Difference between the statement ending balance and the total of the transactions you have checked. As you work, the difference should move toward zero. When you have checked every line on the statement, the difference should read $0.00.
  7. Finish the reconciliation. When the difference is $0.00, select Finish now. QuickBooks saves the reconciliation and offers a reconciliation report. Save or print the report for your records; it is your proof of what cleared, and when.
Note: Transactions that are still outstanding at the bank, such as a check you wrote that has not been cashed yet, stay unchecked. They are not a problem. They carry forward and will clear in a later month.

Troubleshooting

The difference is not zero

Stay on the Reconcile page and compare totals before changing anything. Check the total payments and total deposits QuickBooks shows against the totals on your statement. The side that is off tells you where to look.

  • A checked transaction has the wrong amount. Open it directly from the reconcile screen, correct the amount to match the statement, and save. The difference updates immediately.
  • A transaction was checked that is not on the statement. Uncheck it. Common causes are a duplicate entry, or a transaction dated in this period that actually cleared last month.
  • The difference equals one transaction, or twice one transaction. An equal difference usually means one item was missed or checked in error. A doubled difference usually means an amount was entered with the wrong sign, such as a deposit recorded as a payment.
  • Two digits are swapped. If the difference divides evenly by 9, suspect a transposition, such as 540 entered as 450.

A transaction on the statement is missing in QuickBooks

Do not force the reconciliation. Leave the Reconcile page (your checked items are saved as a draft), add the missing transaction through the bank register or the + New menu with the correct date and amount, then return to Reconcile and resume. Bank fees and interest are the most common missing items; enter them during the reconciliation when prompted, using the account the fee belongs to.

The beginning balance is wrong

A wrong beginning balance means an earlier period changed after it was reconciled: a reconciled transaction was edited, deleted, or had its date moved. Do not edit the statement fields to compensate. Open Accounting, then Chart of accounts, then View register for the account, and look for reconciled transactions (marked R) whose amounts differ from the earlier statement. Restoring the original amount usually restores the beginning balance. In stubborn cases, undo the most recent reconciliation from the account history by account (under Settings, then Reconcile, then History by account) and redo that month in order.

Closing note

Reconcile every bank and credit card account every month, right after the statement closes. A monthly habit keeps each session short, catches bank errors and duplicate entries while they are still easy to fix, and means the balance you see in QuickBooks is a balance you can trust for tax time, loan applications, and everyday decisions.
